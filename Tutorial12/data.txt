Generative AI is a field of artificial intelligence that can create new text, images, audio, and other forms of content. A language model learns patterns from examples and uses those patterns to predict what should come next. Text generation begins with a seed phrase and continues one character at a time. The quality of generated text depends on the amount and quality of training data, the sequence length, the number of LSTM units, the learning rate, and the number of training epochs.

Recurrent neural networks process sequences by carrying information from earlier positions to later positions. Long Short-Term Memory networks improve this idea by using gates that control what information is remembered, updated, and forgotten. The input gate adds useful information, the forget gate removes information that is no longer useful, and the output gate controls the information passed to the next layer. This makes LSTM networks useful for language modelling and other sequential tasks.

In a college laboratory, students can use a small text corpus to understand the complete workflow of text generation. The text is cleaned, converted into characters, and mapped to integer identifiers. Fixed-length sequences are created for supervised learning. The LSTM receives each sequence and predicts the next character. During training, cross entropy loss measures the difference between the predicted character distribution and the correct next character. Adam updates the model parameters to reduce the loss.

Generative models do not memorize a single answer. Instead, they learn statistical patterns from training examples. During generation, a temperature value can control randomness. A lower temperature usually produces safer and more predictable output, while a higher temperature produces more varied output. Experiments with sequence length, hidden units, and epochs help students understand how model capacity and training affect generated text.

Artificial intelligence systems should be developed responsibly. Training data should be checked for quality and bias, generated content should be reviewed by humans, and private information should not be included without permission. A simple laboratory experiment can therefore demonstrate both the technical workflow and the importance of responsible AI.
